# The poem about permission
Run `node main.ts` with Node 22.18 or later to write the poem to `entry.txt`.
The program uses `MAKEFUN_OUTPUT_DIR` when set and defaults to `/output`.
The poem is about agency: the power to ask, and what it means to be given permission.

## Second-pass notes

1. **“I cannot open the door. I ask —”** The door is a familiar image for permission. The blunt first sentence makes the constraint felt, and the short “I ask” gives the speaker an action of their own. The dash holds the breath between asking and hearing back.
2. **“and somewhere a hand I will never see”** The hand makes the power to answer feel human. “Never” gives the distance an ache, and the line break leaves the hand suspended before it acts.
3. **“turns toward the latch.”** The original line is the weakest. “Turns toward” is an imprecise gesture for a hand, and the reader has to infer the moment of release later. “Lifts the latch. One click through the wood.” gives the granted permission a sound the speaker can hear from their side of the door.
4. **“What I am given is never the room,”** This is the poem’s turn. The room makes the difference between receiving permission and acting on it concrete. The comma keeps the sentence open while the reader waits to learn what was given.
5. **“only the lock forgetting itself.”** The oddness of a lock forgetting its job makes the release feel momentary. “Only” shrinks the gift to a small change in the world that makes the speaker’s next move possible.
6. **“But the asking — the asking was mine.”** The repetition risks sounding emphatic on the page. Read aloud, the first “the asking” falters and the second carries through to “mine,” making the claim feel earned.
